How Does Apple Watch Count Stand Hours?

How do Apple Watch stand hours work?

Apple Watch tracks standing by the clock hour: if you stand up and move for at least a minute during an hour, that hour counts as a stand hour.

Apple says: “The blue Stand ring shows how many times in the day you’ve stood and moved for at least 1 minute per hour.”[1]

The Health app also records Stand Minutes: how many minutes in each hour you were standing and moving. Apple explains: “Earning at least one Stand Minute each hour also earns you the hour in your Stand ring.”[2] So whether you stand and move for 1 minute or 40 minutes in an hour, the Stand ring counts it as 1 hour; only your Stand Minutes show the difference.

A stand hour tells you only whether you stood at some point in that hour, not how long you’ve been sitting without a break: get up for a glass of water at 9:00 and the 9:00 hour counts, yet by 10:50 you’ve been sitting for 1 hour 49 minutes straight, and the Stand ring doesn’t show it; see How does the Apple Watch stand reminder count time? How does Apple Watch know you stood up? Does standing still count?

Not necessarily: Apple asks you to stand up and move, not just stand.

Apple’s Close Your Rings page says: “Don’t just stand there. While you’re up, move around a bit. Get some water. Stretch. Visit a coworker.”[3]

In 2024, researchers at the University of Dundee in the UK analyzed August 2022 data from 20 Apple Watch users, about 9,200 hours in all, to see which measures predicted whether an hour counted as a stand hour. Steps and active energy were the strongest predictors: an hour with more than 97.5 steps, or more than 100 kJ (about 24 kcal) of active energy, was much more likely to count as a stand hour. The authors also suggest that whether a stand hour is recorded depends on more than one factor.[4]

What’s the difference between Stand and Exercise on Apple Watch?

The Stand ring counts how many hours in the day you got up and moved; the Exercise ring counts your minutes of activity at or above a brisk walk.

What each of the three Apple Watch Activity rings counts

RingWhat it countsWhat Apple says
Move (red)Active calories burned“The red Move ring shows how many active calories you’ve burned.”[1]
Exercise (green)Minutes at or above a brisk walk“Close your Exercise ring by completing at least 30 minutes of activity at or above a brisk walk.”[3]
Stand (blue)Hours in which you got up and moved“The blue Stand ring shows how many times in the day you’ve stood and moved for at least 1 minute per hour.”[1]

For example, a 30-minute brisk walk from 10:00 to 10:30 adds 30 minutes to the Exercise ring but only 1 hour to the Stand ring, because all 30 minutes fall within the 10:00 hour.

What do 12 stand hours and “4/12” mean on Apple Watch? How do you change the goal?

“4/12” means you’ve earned 4 stand hours today toward a Stand goal of 12.

Apple’s Close Your Rings page uses 12 hours: “Close your Stand ring by getting up and moving around for at least 1 minute during 12 different hours in the day.”[3]

You can set the Stand goal anywhere from 6 to 16 hours.[5] You’ve been able to change it since watchOS 7[6], and since watchOS 11 you can also set a different goal for each day of the week.[7]

  1. Open the Activity app on Apple Watch.
  2. Turn the Digital Crown to the Stand ring, then tap the Change Goals button.
  3. Tap Change for Today or Change Daily Goal, tap the minus or plus button to adjust, then tap Set.

To set a different goal for each day of the week, tap Change Daily Goal, tap the Goal Type button in the top-right corner, then tap Schedule; adjust the goals, then tap Set Schedule.[5]

Why isn’t Apple Watch counting my stand hours?

If you stood up but the hour didn’t count, Apple Watch may not be worn properly, or, as many users report, your arm may have barely moved.

  • If Apple Watch is too loose or too tight, or a shirt sleeve is in the way, it may not detect that it’s on your wrist.[8] Apple warns that if you turn off Wrist Detection, “some Activity measurements will be unavailable.”[8]
  • Carrying only your iPhone, without Apple Watch, doesn’t record standing. The Stand ring appears only if you have an Apple Watch[9], and Apple Watch is what records Stand Minutes[2].
  • Many users report that Apple Watch judges standing mostly from arm swing. Pushing a shopping cart, cooking, or working at a standing desk keeps your arm fairly still, so standing may not count; knitting while seated keeps your arm moving, so it may count as standing.[10]
